

This topic is something that has been stirring around in my head for quite a while now, because I spent a lont time struggling to articulate the *why* in why I liked something. What I've come to settle on is that generally I find a large amount of beauty in not just the quality, whatever it may be, but also in the familiarity of whatever may be around me. 

I started writing this years ago in my head when I often struggled to pick out new clothes. I would spend so much time agonizing over whether or not I liked a particular shirt or hoodie, and that same item I agonized over choosing would become a new favorite weeks or months later. To me this process is really key to everything in my life. I find it's really hard to appreciate say a kitchen knife until I understand exactly how to hold it, how it feels, how it slices potatoes versus carrots. And really as I've spent more time refining my thoughts, it's become even harder to judge art until I've truly sat with it and examined it in every which way.

When I say this applies to everything in my life I also truly mean everything: my car, my couches, the texture of my sheets, the placement of my couches. But furthermore it also applies to people in ways I didn't expect. I've seen many attractive people while living my daily life, but truly *beauty* always comes from a kind of understanding, often with freinds. I also think that this beauty is hard to attain, and it comes from a combination of many small interactions with a person and an understanding of how they think, how they act, what they like, and more. Really a large amount of the beauty I see day to day comes from these kinds of interactions. 

To give some examples, after spending years talking with my friends it becomes fairly easy to understand why someone chose to say what they said at that specific point or rather why they chose to let that silence last just a little longer; But also how they always seem to do so in moments almost identical to this one. Or how I know that my roommate likes it when the blanket is folded, so I can just do it now and save them the trouble. It's these moments of understanding that I find a huge amount of satisfaction and appreciation, and it absolutely drives me to understand more about my tools, the art I keep, and the friends I keep.

The same happiness applies to whenever I see an object that despite being worn is truly loved, and it carries many stories in why it has certain scratches or why the maker chose to make it exactly the way it is. For me that object is the computer I'm currently writing on, as of right now I've had it for almost three years and every time I open it or do an assignment on it I feel a little bit happier. I've spent the time wearing down all of the once textured keycaps, I took the time to switch the operating system and to configure exactly how I want to type so that it feels like second nature. I spent hours choosing the perfect backround picture, the perfect terminal, the perfect settings and the perfcet colors for everything. I'd imagine that people who paint or draw come to feel the same way with certain mediums, brushes, or materials, but I wouldn't know since I find art in other areas.

I want to keep writing for the sake of writing, but if I have to end this little essay I would encourage you to seek out understanding in your everyday life. Read parts of the manual for your car, ask your friend their opinion on something, ask why are your spoons shaped that way and should they be shaped that way; And I think that if you do this for long enough, and make changes or customizations when something doesn't fit, you'll feel a newfound appreciation and satisfaction with everything around you that makes life feel a little better and a little more worth living.
